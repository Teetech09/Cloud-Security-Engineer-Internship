Week 2, Task 07 — Phase A: IAM Enumeration Fundamentals

Objective
Understand IAM entity relationships, enumeration commands, and how
misconfigurations can create privilege escalation opportunities.

IAM Entity Relationships
IAM users can receive permissions directly or through IAM groups.
Roles provide temporary credentials when assumed by trusted principals.
A role's trust policy defines who can assume it, while its permissions
policies define the actions it can perform. Managed policies are separate,
reusable policies; inline policies belong to a specific user, group,
or role. Permissions boundaries limit permissions but do not grant them.

Commands Executed
1. aws iam list-users
   Listed IAM users, including administrative, development, and
   lab-related identities.

2. aws iam list-policies --scope Local
   Listed customer managed policies, including policies with names
   suggesting privilege escalation exercises. Policy names alone do
   not confirm exploitable permissions.

3. aws iam list-roles
   Listed service-linked roles and custom lab roles.

4. aws iam get-role --role-name intern_role
   Confirmed that intern_role trusts the EC2 service through
   sts:AssumeRole. The role has the InternLabRestrictAdminBoundary
   permissions boundary applied. No last-used information was returned.

How Enumeration Supports Privilege Escalation Analysis
Enumeration helps identify identities, policies, and trust relationships
for further review. An attacker with sufficient access could use this
information to look for overly broad policy-management permissions,
unsafe role trust, or permissions to pass powerful roles to services.
The commands executed were read-only and did not themselves change
permissions. No exploitable privilege escalation path was confirmed
from these outputs alone.

Explicit Allow, Implicit Deny, and Explicit Deny
Explicit allow: A matching Allow statement grants permission, subject
to other applicable policy controls.
Implicit deny: Access is denied because the applicable policies do
not provide the required permission.
Explicit deny: A matching Deny statement blocks an action and overrides
an Allow statement.

Key Learning
Trust and permissions must be reviewed separately. A permissions
boundary does not grant access, and its name does not prove which
actions it restricts. Policy documents and applicable controls must
be examined before concluding that an identity has excessive access.
